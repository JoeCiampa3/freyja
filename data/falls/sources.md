# Fall impact sources

The literature supports fall-impact magnitudes properly only for sideways falls onto the hip and forward falls onto the outstretched hands. Even there, almost every human number comes from falls onto padded surfaces from kneeling or reduced heights. Forces measured on volunteers sit at roughly 0.5–1.7 body weights (BW) per hand and 1.3–4.0 kN at the hip (the upper edge is Nankaku's stated 2.0–4.0 kN range, B6/S7). Models and cadaver rigs for rigid floors from standing height give 5.6–10 kN at the hip, or 2.9–4.3 kN for the one model driven by measured human kinematics (S10). Head, trunk and back, elbow and knee have no human-subject force data in any direction.

## TL;DR

- **Hands, forward falls:** measured per-hand peaks run from 0.46 BW (30 cm mats, slowed fall) to 1.7 BW (1 m shoulder-height falls onto padding). Full-text hand numbers: Lo et al. 2003 (F1, 840 N per hand, 1.2 BW, mean of first peaks; mean + SD is 1022 N) and Chiu & Robinovitch 1998 (F4, model, up to 4.2 kN at a 2 m descent). Other Verified rows: hip F6 and S5 (Groen et al. 2010), S1 (Robinovitch et al. 1991, model), S10 (van den Kroonenberg 1995, thesis chapter 3, model) and S11 (Fleps et al. 2018, cadaver, peak 5.6–7.1 kN); shoulder and hand S2–S4 (Sabick et al. 1999); and F9 (a 1.6 kg robot, not transferable).
- **Hip, sideways falls:** human volunteer falls onto mats and mattresses give 1.3–4.0 kN. Cadaver and model estimates for rigid floors from standing give 5.6–10 kN (2.9–4.3 kN for the one model driven by measured kinematics, S10). These differ by 1.4× to 7.5× depending on which edges are compared (Conflict 1), mainly because of surface padding and fall height.
- **Head:** the only magnitudes are from Hybrid III dummies (95% prediction interval 3.9–24.5 kN and 146–502 g, abstract-level) and from robots. Robot head and body accelerations range from 2.3–6.4 G on a 1.6 kg robot to over 100 G on a life-size humanoid (abstract-level). No source gives magnitudes for trunk, back, elbow or knee contact in humans.

## How to read the tables

- **Status column:**
  - "Verified" means I read the number in the paper's own results, table or figure text. Verified model predictions (F4, S1, S10) are the paper's predicted values, not measurements.
  - "Unverified" means I saw it only in the abstract, a search excerpt or a secondary citation.
- **Normalisation:** I normalised to body weight only where the paper gives subject mass. I used BW = mass × 9.81 m/s². I did not normalise where mass was missing ("n/r" = not reported in what I read).
- **Robot figures:** Fujiwara et al. 2002 is a simulation whose forces appear only as plotted traces. The text gives no peak values, so I report the axis full scale only, as a ceiling of the plot. It is not a measured peak.
- **Head impact velocity** is the head's own contact velocity, not fall height.

Cross-cutting gaps:
- No source measured impact durations, except for times to peak on the hand (F4) and descent times.
- No human study reports a standing-height fall onto a rigid floor; ethics restrict all volunteer data to padding or low heights.\[24\]
- Female-specific absolute forces were not obtained. Laing 2006 and Robinovitch 2004 used women but I have only their relative results.
- Ha & Liu 2015 and the Kajita 2016 full text were not read.

## Conflicts (sources differing by more than a factor of two)

1. **Sideways hip force.**
   - Volunteers on padding: 1.33–2.5 kN means or medians, maxima up to 3.1 kN in Groen (F6, forward from standing onto a mattress), and a stated 2.0–4.0 kN range across directions in Nankaku (B6, S7).
   - Rigid-contact predictions and cadaver rigs: 5.6 kN relaxed and 8.6 kN muscle-active (S1, 3.7 m/s), 2.9–4.3 kN for the authors' realistic variant and up to 9.99 kN for the point-mass bound (S10), 5.6–7.1 kN (S11, 3.1 m/s).
   - Ratio 2.2–7.5× between the table's extremes (5.6 kN over 2.5 kN; 9.99 kN over 1.33 kN). Against Nankaku's 4.0 kN upper edge the low-end ratio is only 1.4×, and S10's realistic variant (2.9–4.3 kN) overlaps the padded range, so 9.99 kN is an idealised bound rather than a typical prediction. Likely reasons:
     - 13 cm mattresses and judo mats against rigid ground;
     - kneeling against standing fall heights;
     - trained or voluntary protective responses;
     - models that assume the full effective mass and rigid contact;
     - cadavers lacking active muscle.
   - The FE femoral-neck force (2.11 kN, S12) is an internal bone load, so it does not compare directly with ground contact force.
2. **Forward hand force.**
   - Borrelli 2023: 0.46–0.64 BW. DeGoede 2002: up to 1.7 BW (ratio up to 3.7×). The Chiu & Robinovitch model: up to 4.2 kN for falls up to 2 m.
   - Likely reasons:
     - 30 cm foam mats against 2.4 cm rubber foam;
     - counterweighted, slowed descent;
     - maximum vertical ground reaction force against first-peak Fmax1;
     - model extrapolation to heights above standing.
3. **Hand force by direction.** The sideways one-arm slap (3.78 BW, S4) against forward two-handed arrests (0.46–1.7 BW per hand) differs by 2–8×. One arm takes the whole load from a kneeling side fall, against two arms sharing a forward fall. S4 is read in the full text; the forward values mix full-text (F1) and abstract-level (F2, F3) readings.
4. **Head force within a single dummy study.** 3.9–24.5 kN and 146–502 g (about 6× and 3.4×) depending on scenario, dummy size and posture (F7).\[9\]\[10\] The companion paper's range goes to 64–564 g and 2.6–29.6 kN.\[17\]
5. **Robot acceleration.** Over 100 G (life-size humanoid, Kajita 2016) against 2.3–6.4 G (1.6 kg BioloidGP, F9), a ratio of 15–40×. Likely reasons:
   - robot mass and size;
   - sensor location (head IMU against an unspecified location);
   - surface;
   - whether a protective strategy was used.

   The airbag condition (20–30 G) also falls well below the unprotected value.\[39\]
6. **Pelvis impact velocity.** Robinovitch 1991 (S1) uses 3.7 m/s, free fall from a 0.7 m pelvis height; the 1.40–6.07 m/s in the secondary review is not in the paper. Lab means are 2.08–2.58 m/s (S13), the inverted-pendulum model gives 1.68–3.22 m/s (B5), reconstructions give 0.198–2.52 m/s (B7), van den Kroonenberg's measured kinematics give 2.47–2.93 m/s (S10) and the cadaver rig is at 3.1 m/s (S11). The likely reasons are free fall without credit for protective responses (S1) against measured falls, differing definitions (resultant against vertical, peak against at-contact), fall type, and simulation against measurement.
