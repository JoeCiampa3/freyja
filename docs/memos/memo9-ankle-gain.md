## Findings

1. **The ankle gain is a lever, and the lever is the stand pose's pitch.** `A_filtered`'s peak ankle torque per kg is 1.27 and 1.26 times the dataset's at the stride's torque peak (geometric mean over 938 and 1038 stride-limbs). Split into factors at that frame: force 0.991 and 0.974, foot weight and inertia 0.998, the dataset's own plate-and-marker torque against its inverse dynamics 1.022 and 1.025, the leg ratio 0.962 (what a geometrically similar body gets), and **the lever 1.302 and 1.313**. The product is 1.266 and 1.258. The lever is long because Freyja's ankle sits 42 and 44 mm further back than the scaled subject's relative to the hip midpoint at the torque peak, and 22 to 32 mm further back at every tenth of the stride; her thigh and shank are rotated back by 2.76 and 2.40 degrees (1.4 m/s; 2.78 and 2.45 at 1.6) from the dataset's, constant through the stride. `retarget.py:330` builds the root orientation as `R_stand @ (the dataset's pelvis rotation)`, and `stand_static`'s root pitch is +2.608 degrees (forward lean; its ankle joint is +2.608), so the model's pelvis pitch is the dataset's plus 2.608 degrees, not "the dataset's absolute pitch" that the comment at `retarget.py:238` to `240` says `pitch_mean: measured` keeps. The comment's own reason, that lever arms of a measured ground reaction are consistent with the data only when the measured pitch is kept, is the thing that fails. The CoP itself is exact (A's CoP relative to the hip midpoint minus the leg ratio times the dataset's: 0.000 mm, maximum 0.000 mm over every stride).
2. **Cancelling the 2.608 degrees removes the ankle gain.** With the existing `pelvis_pitch_offset_deg` knob at -2.608 (the stand pose's own pitch, not tuned; the spec is untouched), the leg orientation differences become -0.17 and +0.20 degrees (thigh, shank), the lever factor 1.065 and 1.080, the ankle slope 0.990 and 0.998 (from 1.185 and 1.188), the stride-peak ratio 1.014 and 1.024 (from 1.245 and 1.249), the mean peak difference +0.027 and +0.042 N m/kg (from 0.409 and 0.425). It is a counterfactual, not a fix: the same run moves the knee (slope 0.949 to 1.031 and 0.960 to 1.046; mean peak difference +0.085 to +0.235 and +0.086 to +0.257 N m/kg) and leaves the sagittal hip (0.680 to 0.676, 0.689 to 0.684) and hip ab/adduction (1.175 and 1.162, unchanged) where they were. A 2.608 degree rotation about the hip midpoint of a 0.8 m leg is 36 mm at the ankle, which is the gap measured.
3. **Force, inertia and the dataset's own consistency do not carry it.** Per kg, A's force equals the measured force through the mass ratio and a filter, so it is lower, not higher: the vertical stride peak is 0.968 and 0.948 of the raw plate's, and at the torque peak 0.982 and 0.970. The foot's weight and inertia is -0.004 N m/kg of 2.08 and 2.14 at the peak frame (0.2%) and at most 0.8% and 0.9% anywhere in stance. The dataset's own inverse-dynamics ankle torque (1.655 and 1.700 N m/kg) equals its plate force through the marker lever (1.684 and 1.741) to 2% (geometric mean 1.022 and 1.025, median 1.011 and 1.017), so the dataset's lever is what the markers say and the excess is Freyja's.
4. **The reference path of the CoP is not the cause.** With the foot-frame CoP mapping (T3b step 0c) the ankle slope is 1.160 and 1.166 (against 1.185 and 1.188), gain 1.217 and 1.227 (1.230 and 1.233), correlation 0.989 and 0.987, and the lever factor stays 1.289 and 1.338. The gain persists. Its source there is the foot, not the hip reference (finding 7, not isolated).
5. **Across subjects the ankle slope follows the lever, the foot length and, weakly, the leg ratio** (n = 17, so an interval of roughly plus or minus 0.4 around any r): with the ankle lever factor r +0.66 and +0.70 (95% interval +0.27 to +0.87 and +0.33 to +0.88), the ankle's forward gap -0.65 and -0.66, the subject's foot length -0.60 and -0.58 (-0.84 to -0.16 and -0.83 to -0.13), the leg ratio +0.45 and +0.43 (both intervals include 0), the mass ratio -0.15 and -0.15, the mean pelvis pitch +0.16 and +0.22 (include 0). The lever correlation is partly built in (the slope and the lever factor come from the same torque); the 2.608 degree offset is the same for every subject, which is why the pelvis pitch does not correlate.
6. **Hip ab/adduction (gain 1.27) is not the pitch; it is the lateral geometry.** Cancelling the pitch leaves it at 1.175 and 1.162. The foot-frame mapping turns it to 0.874 and 0.879. Freyja's hip half-separation is 92.3 mm against 73.3 mm for the subjects scaled by their leg ratio (76.3 as measured), and her hip joint is 832 to 856 mm above the floor against 777 to 797 scaled (+7%). At the torque peak A's ab/adduction torque is 99% the horizontal-force term (0.986 of 0.995 N m/kg); the chain gives lever 1.395 and 1.393, force 1.067 and 1.086, inertia 0.972 and 0.963, closure 0.974 and 0.954. I did not isolate it further (finding 8).
7. **Sagittal hip (slope 0.68) is two things, neither the pitch.** By term, the slope of A on the dataset (1.4 m/s, per kg, frames pooled): total 0.680 (correlation 0.88); in stance 0.797; in swing 0.416 (correlation 0.94, rms 0.19 against 0.39 N m/kg), where the torque is only the weight and inertia of the distal leg; the stance ground term 0.498 (correlation 0.73). A's swing-phase hip torque is 0.42 of the dataset's inverse-dynamics torque at the same correlation. Leg masses as a fraction of body mass: Freyja thigh 0.1461, shank 0.0450, foot 0.0100; the subjects' OpenSim models (mean of 17) femur 0.1258, tibia 0.0502, foot 0.0212 (talus, calcn, toes). Shank plus foot is 0.0550 against 0.0714. A lumped estimate I did by hand (mass times the squared distance from the hip, at 0.57 and 0.84 m for Freyja's shank and foot and 0.6 and 0.85 m for the OpenSim ones; not computed by the script) gives a leg inertia about the hip of about 0.70 of the dataset's, which is not 0.42; the rest is unexplained.
8. **What I did not isolate:** why the foot-frame mapping keeps a lever excess (candidate: Freyja's ankle is 165 mm behind the front of her foot box and 62.7 mm above the floor, against 138 and 49 mm for the subjects scaled, 20 and 28% more), the lateral hip geometry in finding 6, the sagittal hip's swing torque at 0.42, and the 24 mm by which Freyja's ankle is lower than the scaled subject's after the pitch is cancelled (her reach from hip midpoint to ankle is 2.7 to 3.2% longer than the leg ratio times the subject's: 0.973 and 0.977 against 0.947; the ratio uses an estimated leg length, stature times a fraction).

## Tolerances and choices

| item | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| peak frame | the largest \|torque\| of A within that limb's stance (A's ground force above zero) | one frame for every quantity, so the factors multiply; the stride-peak ratio is printed beside it |
| terms worker against `run_stride` | 1e-9 N m, both CoP mappings | same arithmetic; the split inertial minus Jacobian terms is exact to 1e-12 |
| correlation interval | Fisher transform, z = 1.96 | n = 17; stated as approximate |
| cancelling pitch | the stand pose's own root pitch, -2.608 degrees | measured from `stand_static`, not tuned to a result; the existing `pelvis_pitch_offset_deg` knob, spec untouched |
| ankle joint centre from the marker | the subject's OpenSim offset in the tibia frame, tibia taken as upright | the tibia is inclined by up to about 30 degrees at push-off; the offset is up to 30 mm, so the error is a few mm typically and up to 15 mm in the worst case; the closure factor (median 1.011) is the empirical check on the dataset's lever |
| hip joint centre | pelvis pose plus the subject's OpenSim hip offsets, pelvis not rolled or yawed | roll and yaw are a few degrees on a 73 to 92 mm half separation, under 5 mm |
| foot length, ankle height of subjects | mid-stance frames 10 to 29 of the stride | a flat-foot window I chose; not tested for sensitivity |
| the gap by tenths of the stride | the middle frame of each tenth | arbitrary |

No band, check tolerance, geometry value or default was touched.
