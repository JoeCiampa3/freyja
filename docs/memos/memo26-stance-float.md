## Decision summary

- **The float is made by the root-height rule and the rigid, flat toe, not by the ankle height or the pelvis tilt.** `retarget.Cycle` (root rule `pelvis_dataset`) takes the dataset's hip height and shifts it by one constant per stride so that the lowest foot-box corner of either foot at any of the 100 phases touches z = 0. In **468 of 469 strides at 1.4 m/s that corner is the flat toe tip of the stance foot in late stance** (push-off, foot pitched toe-down, toe held flat); on the Stage 2 model it was the front of the 165 mm foot box (466 of 469). The body is lifted until that tip clears the floor, and every other phase floats by the amount the tip dips. The tip is now 195 mm ahead of the ankle, 30 mm further than the old box, and the mean float over stance frames went from 17.6 to 31.4 mm (+13.8).
- **Counterfactuals on the new model** (mean float over stance frames, mm; reference 31.4): Stage 2 box restored (41.25 to 165 mm, no toe box) **17.1**; toe box removed (front at the 128 mm hinge) 10.3; heel moved to 41.25 mm 31.9; ankle height 62.7 mm 32.0; frame offset `none` 31.4. So the foot and toe geometry account for the whole increase (-14.3 mm on restoring the old box), the ankle height for **-0.6 mm** (memo 25 said "a fifth": that was wrong, and is retracted), the heel for -0.5, the pelvis frame offset for 0.0, everything else for -0.5.
- **Method A is sensitive to the float.** Applying the measured force at the stance sole instead of at the floor plane (`dynamics.solve_given` takes a floor point; the foot is 20 to 50 mm above it) changes A_filtered's 1.4 m/s p95 by +5.1% (hip right), -5.3% (knee right), -2.2% (ankle right) and the maxima by under 2%. The band is passed both ways and improves: knee RMS 14.3 to 12.7% of peak-to-peak, ankle 8.5 to 7.4%. The sensitivity is not zero: a 5% effect on the p95s (two just over the 5% flag), under 2% on the maxima.
- **The planning claim in T4 is confirmed for the force and approximately for the CoP.** B's total ground force equals the measured force plus A's root residual force to 1.13 N (0.2% of body weight, the toe cycle difference); A's single-support root residual is 98 N mean (15.3% BW), 195 N p95 (30.7% BW). The CoP shift that closes A's residual moment is **-58.2 mm** (x mean, behind), against B's **-63.3 mm** (the record's figure, reproduced to the digit).
- Decisions for you: (1) the root rule: do you want a trial of a rule that places the root with the toe at its rolling angle, so the push-off tip no longer sets the height (proposal 1 below; recommended, as a diagnostic run first); (2) whether A's loads for sizing should be taken with the force at the sole (recommended: report both until the rule is settled); (3) the snapshot I was told about is not in the repository (below).
