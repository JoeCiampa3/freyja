# Stage 2 brief (for Claude Code): level-walking loads

Read `CONVENTIONS.md`, the Stage 1, 1b and 1c briefs, and `docs/memos/memo3-stage1-recap.md` first.

Goal: walking loads at 1.4 and 1.6 m/s on Freyja's own mass distribution, driven by measured human kinematics (the Camargo et al. 2021 open dataset, Georgia Tech), computed three ways and compared. Freyja's mass data is currently human-based, so agreement between the methods and the dataset's own joint moments is a soft check on the pipeline. Once CAD numbers replace the anthropometry, disagreement will be legitimate rather than a bug, so the baseline is recorded now and pinned to the snapshot hash (T6).

Methods, all on the same retargeted kinematics:
- **B (primary).** Freyja's own dynamics. The root rows of inverse dynamics give the net wrench the ground must supply; the centre of pressure is solved from moment balance, so the root rows close by construction (except the vertical-axis moment).
- **A (cross-check).** The measured ground reaction force and CoP, scaled to Freyja. The root rows will not close; the residual measures how far her motion is from the subject's.
- **H (cross-check).** Total force from Freyja's own CoM motion, applied at the measured CoP. The force rows close; the moment rows leave a residual.

Out of scope: stairs, ramps, sit-to-stand, overground walking, running, toes, arm swing, the closed-loop controller (Joe's own project), actuator selection, any change to the sheet, the template or `pre_processor`.

## Decisions already made

B primary, A and H as cross-checks (C, a whole-body controller, is Joe's later project). Double support: vertical force split proportional to the CoM's position between the feet as the nominal rule, plus a bound that sweeps the split over its feasible range and keeps the worst case per joint. Treadmill walking first. Arms at the sides with no swing, recorded as an assumption in every record. Envelope statistic: the 95th percentile across subject-strides, with the maximum shown beside it (Joe may override).

Decided after `dee54b0` and `f84571f` (Joe confirms by committing this brief; each line is a one-line revert if he disagrees):

- **Trunk.** The spec key `trunk` is `rigid` or `counter_rotate`. `rigid`: spine and neck held at the standing pose relative to the pelvis, so the trunk follows the pelvis's roll and yaw. `counter_rotate`: the lumbosacral lateral-bending and axial-rotation joints cancel the pelvis roll and yaw, so abdomen, thorax and head hold their standing roll and yaw in the world frame; sagittal motion stays rigid with the pelvis. T3c.2 (trunk IMU; the dataset has no shoulder or trunk markers) found the real trunk follows 57 to 63% of the pelvis roll and 76 to 93% of its yaw, with phase lags of 65 to 116 degrees. Neither mode is exact, and no scalar fraction represents a quarter-cycle lag, so no representation parameter is adopted. Both modes stay as the bracket. `counter_rotate` remains the headline for B and H's lateral and yaw-sensitive rows because it reproduces the measured lateral force (correlation 0.86 against 0.08 at 1.4 m/s). That fit was found by comparing with the measured force, so it is not a validation of the cancellation. Joints above the pelvis depend only on the kinematics and the trunk mode (the foot Jacobians have no columns there), so their rows are the bracket across modes plus one human-like reference point (T9.1).
- **Pelvis pitch.** `pitch_mean` is `measured` (each subject's cycle-mean sagittal pelvis pitch, anterior positive) or a number in degrees. A and H apply a measured force and CoP that belong to the subject's measured posture, so a different pitch makes them a hybrid and not a load. `measured` is therefore the only value allowed for A and H and for every verification; a number is allowed for B and for the sensitivity tables, and any A or H record with a number is flagged `hybrid`. The IK pitch (what the dataset's own inverse dynamics used) is the reference for A, H and the headline B: with `counter_rotate`, a 5 degree change either way worsens B's single-support outside distance by 12 mm. The IK pitch disagrees with the ASIS and PSIS markers by about 12 degrees for AB12 and AB19 (6 subjects checked), so flagged subjects are reported with and without, like the offset subjects (T9.3).
- **Double support.** A frame is feasible when the total CoP, which the root rows fix independently of how the force is split, lies in the convex hull of the two feet's contact polygons. The force-split bound does not size loads: it lies within -2 to +7 N m of the nominal torque at the 95th percentile because the peaks are single-support peaks (T3c.3). It stays in the record; no more work on it.
- **Contact-polygon band.** `SEGMENT_TOL_M` = 25 mm defines which vertices form a contact polygon (a geometric definition, equal to the hull band). It is not an acceptance threshold. Feasibility is reported as outside-distance distributions beside the measured-CoP baseline, never as pass or fail.
- **Ankle inversion.** The dataset's moment is about an oblique subtalar axis and Freyja's `ankle_ie` is a pure anteroposterior axis, so the two are not comparable and no method agrees (correlation about -0.1, which is not a sign flip). It leaves the agreement table, with the reason written there. It is reported as a load from A, B and H, flagged `unverified` and `not comparable with the dataset`; its sign mapping rests on the kinematic mapping and the hip ab/adduction and rotation agreement, not on a dataset moment.
- **Toe.** No model change in Stage 2. A rigid, toeless box cannot represent late-stance push-off: in the last decile of stance the measured CoP lies outside that foot's polygon in 66 to 85% of single-support frames and 99 to 100% of double-support frames. A toe joint is a hardware decision, so Stage 2 supplies the demand numbers and a proposed decision record (T9.4) and Joe decides.
- **Envelope torque and power rows.** Band: correlation at least 0.9 and RMS difference at most 15% of peak-to-peak for the sagittal hip, knee and ankle moments. `A_filtered` meets it (0.91 to 0.995; 10.9 to 14.7%). `A_native`, H and B are judged against the same band in T6 and enter only if they meet it. Rows from `A_filtered` are flagged provisional (A borrows the human CoP; its agreement verifies the pipeline against the dataset's own inverse dynamics and does not validate Freyja's loads), with `A_native`'s maximum shown beside as the with-impact bound. Hip ab/adduction and hip rotation fall outside the band (`A_filtered` correlation 0.97 and 0.86, RMS 18% for both): they are listed with their agreement numbers and flagged `outside band`, and the band is not widened after seeing them; if Joe wants them admitted, that is a new decision with those numbers in front of it. Joints above the pelvis: kinematics-only rows (the bracket); arms excluded because arm swing is not modelled.

## T3. Dynamic wrench solver (B)

`sim/scripts/dynamics.py`, generalising `statics.py` to frames with non-zero `qvel` and `qacc`. Per frame, with contacts and joint-limit constraints both disabled (`mjDSBL_CONTACT` and `mjDSBL_LIMIT`, restored afterwards: a stop would otherwise carry load and understate the required torque on frames past a limit), `mj_inverse` gives `qfrc_inverse`. The root rows give the net ground wrench. Joint torque is the joint rows of `qfrc_inverse - qfrc_ground`. Single support: the total force acts at a CoP on the floor plane, solved from the two horizontal-moment equations; the vertical-axis moment is left in the root rows and reported. Double support: the proportional split of force (and of horizontal force), each foot's CoP at the same normalised offset along its own box with a shared lateral shift, both solved from the two moment equations. Swing foot: zero. Feasibility, reported per frame and never clipped: the CoP must lie inside the hull of the foot-box vertices within a named band (default 25 mm) of that foot's own lowest point, projected onto the floor, so a foot that floats slightly still has a contact polygon; the friction ratio must be below the model's `mu`. The double-support bound sweeps the force split over its feasible range and keeps the worst case per joint.

First report, before any joint torque is computed: Freyja's total ground force from her own CoM motion (`m(a+g)`, from the root force rows) against the measured force scaled to her weight, per phase. Compare like for like: the measured force goes through the same zero-lag filter as the kinematics (report the raw comparison beside it), because kinematics filtered at 6 Hz cannot contain force content above 6 Hz. Add a ratio-versus-cutoff curve (6, 8, 10, 12 and 15 Hz, measured force filtered identically). The force depends on the kinematics alone, so this tests the root trajectory before any moment error can hide it. If the like-for-like peak vertical force differs by more than 15% (my review threshold, not an acceptance limit), or the fore-aft peak-to-peak force does, or either time-course has a correlation below 0.85, stop and report. If the fore-aft force is short, diagnose in this order: the filter-matched comparison; whether the shortfall per subject tracks the leg-length ratio; then a third root rule whose horizontal motion is driven by the stance foot's contact with the belt, which removes slip by construction. Skip the arm-swing diagnostic: the two arms swing in opposite phase and cancel in the CoM force; they matter for yaw angular momentum, later.

Tests first:
- A single rigid body on a foot box with prescribed sway reproduces the ZMP formula for the CoP to 1e-9, with and without rotational inertia.
- In free fall the root rows are zero.
- Symmetric double support with the CoM centred gives an equal split.
- Flipping the sign of the ground wrench breaks the root-row residual, and the test detects it.
- A pose beyond a hinge limit gives the same torque as a copy of the model with that range removed.
- Independent check: the hip, knee and ankle torques on five frames equal a bottom-up Newton-Euler recursion written in the test, using the solved ground force and MuJoCo kinematics but not `mj_inverse`, to 1e-6 relative.

## T5. Batch runner and record

`fy gait <spec>` reads `sim/scenarios/gait/walk_treadmill.yaml` (dataset, speeds, subject selection, stride cap per subject, methods, smoothing, the upper-body assumption, the double-support rule) and runs every subject-stride. It writes `sim/gait/<id>/gait.json` (untracked) and a deterministic `GAIT.md` under 400 lines whose header carries the dataset attribution line.
